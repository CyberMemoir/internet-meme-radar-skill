# Platform Methods

## General Method

For any platform, prioritize:

1. User-provided links, screenshots, captions, comments, or sounds.
2. Official APIs and allowed public pages.
3. Public search results and reputable explainers.
4. Cross-platform examples from unrelated communities.

Avoid restricted scraping or stealth automation.

## Trend Signals

Stronger signals:

- Same phrase appears in many recent captions or comments.
- Same sound/template is reused by different creators.
- Comments repeat the same joke structure.
- Parodies, remixes, duets, stitches, reaction edits, or imitation posts exist.
- Hashtags emerge around the phrase/template.
- Users explain the joke to each other.
- The format spreads beyond the original creator or fandom.
- It appears across platforms or communities.

Weaker signals:

- Only one creator uses it.
- Engagement comes from creator fame, not template adoption.
- Comments do not repeat or recognize the phrase.
- It looks like an ad or coordinated campaign.
- It is an inside joke for one small group.
- It is old and mostly used nostalgically or ironically.

## Douyin

Preferred data order:

1. User-provided Douyin links.
2. User-provided screenshots.
3. User-provided captions or comment samples.
4. Official Douyin Open Platform APIs when available.
5. Public search results that are allowed to access.
6. Ask the user to search and send top results, captions, screenshots, or comment samples.

Do not bypass login, signatures, CAPTCHAs, device checks, or risk-control systems.

## TikTok / YouTube Shorts / Instagram Reels

Focus on sound reuse, hashtag repetition, creator diversity, comment adoption, stitching/duet/remix behavior, and whether the format appears outside a single creator's fanbase.

## Bilibili / Xiaohongshu / Weibo / Kuaishou

Pay attention to platform-specific language. A phrase may be mainstream on one platform and niche or stale on another. Check whether the usage is tied to fandom, regional speech, beauty/fashion/product notes, danmu/comment culture, or reposted short-video content.
